## Why (the measured case)

Three independent instruments say our WEIGHTS, not our search or features, are
the deficit — all in ARCHON's own search at fixed 110k nodes:

| weights | Elo vs D48X | n |
|---|---|---|
| Stockfish `eca43a97` | **+90.7 ± 16.9** | 380 |
| Reckless 0.9.0 (768-wide) | **+30.0 ± 18.0** | 406 |
| our net, de-framed (null) | −12.0 ± 18.2 | 406 |

and `sf-features` proved our HalfKA + FullThreats indices are IDENTICAL to
SF's (40/40 × 2 perspectives), so it is not the feature set.

**The defect.** The trainer builds `target = wdl*result + (1-wdl)*eval_t`.
`result` is an expected score (draw = 0.5). `eval_t` is
`sigmoid(score/400)` — a win probability with **no draw model**. The two
halves are different quantities and we average them. At +174 the eval half
says 0.607 where real outcomes score 0.75; the blended label is ~0.65. The
error is symmetric about 0.5, so it CANCELS IN THE MEAN and does not cancel in
the FUNCTION: every position is pulled toward 0.5 and the learned curve is
systematically flatter than reality. Invisible to rank-correlation tests
(ranking is scale-invariant), which is why our net looked fine on those.

**The fix already exists in the code**, unused: `features.py:545-552`,
`TrainConfig.target_offset` (`spec.py:309`), default `0.0` = the broken branch.
Measured on our own corpora, held out: Brier **0.0354 vs 0.0532**.

**The irony in D48X's own config:** `teacher_frame_offset: 163.0,
teacher_frame_scale: 76.9` — its teacher WAS trained in the calibrated frame,
and we mapped the teacher DOWN into the plain-sigmoid label frame to make them
agree. We converted the good frame into the bad one.

## Why per-corpus

The transform is not a property of positions; it is a property of the LABELLING
PROCESS and the POPULATION — label depth, who played the games out, and the
position mix all change what "score s" predicts about the outcome. Our own
measured fits disagree by >2x in scale (uho a=175.5 b=71.9; fishpack32
a=160.2 b=33.2). One global transform over a 12-corpus mix is a compromise that
is wrong for every component.

**Confound to rule out first:** a scale difference can also be a UNITS
difference (the `SFBINPACK_SCORE_DIV` frame bug class). The training study
tested frame-mixing across 11 corpora and called it a null (±13%), but a 2x
gap exceeds that null, so the fits get a units sanity check before use.

## Plan

1. **Fit** `(a_i, b_i)` per corpus by MLE, held out, bootstrapped
   (`tools/nnue/fit_target_transform.py`).
2. **Sanity-check** the spread: units confound vs genuine predictiveness.
3. **Thread per-corpus params** through `make_mixed_batches`. This is cheap
   because each batch is drawn from exactly ONE corpus (`data.py:95-130`), so
   the corpus index is known at map time.
4. **Verify calibration** post-transform: every corpus's targets should agree

   pinned at `wdl 0.3 / scale 400 / offset 0`, which would score a calibrated

### CP-SCALE RISK — one scalar is NOT enough, computed ahead of the net

Setting the scale is trivial (an i32 at CENN byte 44, automated). The risk is
whether a SCALAR can absorb the new transform at all. It cannot.

The head learns `out ≈ logit(target)`, cp = `out × network_scale`. Under the OLD
transform `logit(sigmoid(s/400)) × 400 = s` EXACTLY — which is why
network_scale is 400 and why a scalar always sufficed. Under the new
draw-aware transform the implied cp per unit of true score is not flat:

| s | uho (25%) | fishpack32 (16%) | farseerT74 (12%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 0.0043 | 0.0013 | 0.0054 |

### ★★★ THIRD OOM — and the cgroup guard did NOT prevent it. Root cause found.

Trainer killed at **06:28:44** (pid 1020872) during the study-15 gate build,
despite that build running inside `systemd-run --scope -p MemoryMax=5G`.

**Why the cgroup failed:** it caps the BUILD. The build stayed under 5 GB while
TOTAL system demand still exhausted RAM, so the GLOBAL OOM killer ran and chose
its victim by score — and the kernel line names the reason:

```
Killed process 1020872 (python) ... oom_score_adj:200
```

**Everything in this session's process tree inherits `oom_score_adj=200`**, and
the trainer is the largest RSS in that group, so it is the *preferred* victim
every time. The cgroup was protecting the wrong process.

**The real fix, and it needs no root:** an unprivileged process may RAISE its
own `oom_score_adj` but not lower it. So do not try to protect the trainer —
**make every build and match MORE killable than it**:

```bash
systemd-run --user --scope -p MemoryMax=5G -p OOMScoreAdjust=1000 <cmd>
# or, inside a wrapper:  echo 1000 > /proc/self/oom_score_adj; exec <cmd>
```

With adj=1000 the build is chosen first and dies alone; the trainer at 200
survives. **This is the guard that should have been used from the first
incident.** Cgroup + OOMScoreAdjust together, not cgroup alone.

**Second lesson — checkpoints can be TORN.** sb160 was written 2/4 (raw +
quant, no ema pair) because the kill landed mid-write, and resuming from it
died with `FileNotFoundError`. **Always resume from the highest COMPLETE 4-file
set**, and delete torn ones. Resumed from sb150; the torn sb160 pair removed.

Cost of this incident: 21 superbatches (sb150→171) plus a fresh-Adam transient.
Running total lost to OOM across three incidents: ~4 + 7 + 21 = **32
superbatches**, roughly 70 minutes of GPU.

### ★ THE OOM GUARD, CORRECTED AND VERIFIED — `tools/oomlast.sh`

My previous fix did NOT work. **`systemd-run --user --scope -p
OOMScoreAdjust=1000` silently has no effect** — verified 2026-07-29: the build
launched under it still read `oom_score_adj=200`, identical to the trainer.
Nor can you patch it afterwards: `cargo`/`rustc` children are transient and
gone before you can write to their `/proc` entry.

**Working fix — set it once, before exec, so children inherit at fork:**

```sh
tools/oomlast.sh <cmd>                                    # or
systemd-run --user --scope -p MemoryMax=5G tools/oomlast.sh <cmd>
```

Verified: wrapped process reads **adj=1000**, its child **inherits 1000**, and
the trainer stays at **200** — so the kernel picks the build first and the
trainer survives.

**The reasoning, recorded so it is not re-derived:** everything in this
session's process tree inherits `oom_score_adj=200`, and the trainer is the
largest RSS among them, so it is the preferred victim every time. An
unprivileged process may RAISE its own `oom_score_adj` but never lower it — so
you cannot protect the trainer, only make everything else more killable.

**Use `tools/oomlast.sh` for EVERY build, match and sweep from here.** Cgroup
`MemoryMax` alone is not enough: it bounds a runaway but does nothing when
total demand simply exceeds RAM, which is what killed the trainer all three

let the distribution go where it likes, and quantise post hoc.

the target-transform work are ALL computed on the FLOAT net. **We play with the
QUANTISED net.** Nothing in the training loop, and nothing in any screen we run
before packing, can see this damage. It also explains the Reckless result
directly — a smaller net trained with quantisation in the loop beating a larger
one quantised afterwards.

**STATUS: HYPOTHESIS, NOT A MEASUREMENT.** This measures weight PRECISION, not
Elo. The link to strength is an inference and must not be quoted as a finding.
