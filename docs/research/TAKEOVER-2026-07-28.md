## 2d. THE ACTUAL SEARCH DEFECT: A TILT, NOT A LEVEL

Two facts that only mean something together (`shape.json`, 25 UNCONVERGED positions,
both engines to fixed d20):

- At **equal depth we use FEWER nodes than SF** (median 0.921) ⇒ at equal **nodes we are
  DEEPER than SF** — and still wrong more often (13/25 vs 16/25 on the oracle move).
  **Deeper and wronger is a SHAPE defect, not a strength deficit.**
- Per-depth width ratio (ours/SF) falls monotonically across the tree:

| depth | 6–10 | 12–14 | 15–18 | 19–20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ours ÷ SF | **1.29–1.45** | 1.56–1.66 | 1.05–1.19 | **0.90–0.92** |

  Crossover ≈ d18. Our EBF at d18–19 is **below** SF's (1.333/1.363 vs 1.567/1.522):
  at exactly the depths where sharp positions resolve, we grow the tree more slowly.
  Per-position: tilt present in **17/25**, median 1.35 (quartiles 0.88/1.35/2.46) — a
  majority effect, heterogeneous.

**This is why every arm all session landed in the null.** `RED_TABLE_NUM` alone,
`EVAL_SCALE`, LMR-off — all move the LEVEL, shifting both ends together. The defect is
the TILT between them, which no global knob can express.

`reductions[i] = (RED_TABLE_NUM·ln(i) + RED_TABLE_OFFSET) / RED_TABLE_DEN`, and
`scale = reductions[depth] · reductions[move_number]`. So **NUM is the log SLOPE**
(dominates at high depth) and **OFFSET is the CONSTANT** (dominates at low depth).
Currently NUM=2834, DEN=128, **OFFSET=0** — the tilt knob has never been moved.
Candidate arm, pinned to hold mid-depth reduction fixed at ln(i)≈2.5:
**NUM 1984 + OFFSET 2125** (≈ +18% reduction shallow, −12.5% deep). It shares its NUM
with the running LMR−30% match, so the pair differs in exactly one parameter.
Watch out: OFFSET≠0 makes `reductions[1]` nonzero, which the code comments currently
rely on being exactly 0 — arm-verify before games (`armverify_tilt.py`).

### The open question this creates (highest-value lead in the file)

**WHY does the in-game search miss what a cold search at the same node count finds?**
The cold search differs from the in-game one in exactly four ways — each discrete and
testable, none yet tested:

1. **TT state** carried from previous moves (cold = empty).
2. **History / correction tables** warmed by the game (cold = zeroed).
3. **Aspiration window** seeded from the previous move's score (cold = fresh).
4. **Soft-stop time allocation** ending the iteration early (cold = fixed budget).

This is the first failure mode of the night that is NOT search strength, NOT eval, NOT
the frame, and NOT reductions. Test it by replaying real lost games through the engine
with each of the four restored/removed one at a time — discrete outcome (does it play
the right move), so no cp instrument and no frame exposure.
